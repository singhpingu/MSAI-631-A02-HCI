AdaptiveStudy AI — source and AI assistance credits
Version 1.0.0, October 11, 2026

This is an original student implementation prepared for Yasharth Singh.
No third-party application or source-code template was copied or bundled.
The six study questions, explanatory examples, dataset generator, logistic
regression implementation, UI, adaptation policy, server, and tests are original.
The original project code is distributed under LICENSE.txt. External resources
retain their own copyright; the archive contains links and citations, not copies
of their papers or implementations.

Conceptual and scholarly sources

Amershi, S., Weld, D., Vorvoreanu, M., Fourney, A., Nushi, B., Collisson, P.,
Suh, J., Iqbal, S. T., Bennett, P. N., Inkpen, K., Teevan, J., Kikin-Gil, R.,
& Horvitz, E. (2019). Guidelines for human-AI interaction. In Proceedings of
the 2019 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems (Article 3,
pp. 1–13). Association for Computing Machinery.
https://doi.org/10.1145/3290605.3300233
Used for disclosure, user control, correction, and explaining AI behavior.

Gajos, K. Z., Weld, D. S., & Wobbrock, J. O. (2010). Automatically generating
personalized user interfaces with Supple. Artificial Intelligence, 174,
910–950.
https://www.eecs.harvard.edu/~kgajos/papers/2010/gajos10automatically.shtml
Used as a research example of personalized interfaces. No Supple code reused.

Horvitz, E. (1999). Principles of mixed-initiative user interfaces. In
Proceedings of the SIGCHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems
(pp. 159–166). Association for Computing Machinery.
https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/principles-mixed-initiative-user-interfaces/
Used for the relationship between automated assistance, timing, and user choice.

Jameson, A. (2008). Adaptive interfaces and agents. In A. Sears & J. A. Jacko
(Eds.), The human-computer interaction handbook: Fundamentals, evolving
technologies and emerging applications (2nd ed., pp. 433–458). CRC Press.
https://media.contaction.li/homepage-2017/abs/Jameson08Handbook.html
Used for adaptive interface concepts and usability tradeoffs.

Ng, A. (2012). CS229 lecture notes: Supervised learning [Lecture notes].
Stanford University.
https://cs229.stanford.edu/notes_archive/cs229-notes-all/cs229-notes1.pdf
Used for logistic regression and gradient-based training concepts.
The Python and JavaScript implementations and synthetic label policy are original.
